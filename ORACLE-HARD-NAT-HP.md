# Assisted NAT traversal method for Endpoint-Dependent Mapping NATs

## Abstract

Some NAT traversal cases remain difficult, especially when both peers are behind NAT devices with Endpoint-Dependent Mapping and unpredictable port allocation.
This case is rare in practice, but technically interesting because it represents one of the hardest NAT traversal scenarios.

This document presents an assisted method for this harder case, based on ICMP probe traffic generated by cooperating third-party systems.
The method can establish direct connections with high probability, but its requirements make it less suitable as a generic NAT traversal method.
It is most practical for bulk transfers that can tolerate long connection establishment times, or when a relay connection can be used first and upgraded to a direct connection in the background.

## NAT Types

As described in [RFC5128](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5128.html), NAT devices may use different mapping and filtering behaviours.
This document focuses on the harder case where both NAT devices use Endpoint-Dependent Mapping and assign external ports unpredictably.

For the purpose of this document, let's assume that both NAT devices also have Endpoint-Dependent Filtering and require a full match on the 5-tuple[^1] to let traffic pass.

## Classic (non)solution

The birthday-paradox method described in the [Tailscale blog article](https://tailscale.com/blog/how-nat-traversal-works) works only while the relevant NAT translation mappings remain alive.
That assumption is easy to miss, but it is critical.

In the double Endpoint-Dependent Mapping case, both peers need to search for each other's unpredictable external ports at the same time.
The number of required probes grows quickly, while the useful lifetime of each mapping remains short, in many cases as short as 30 seconds.
As a result, the classic method does not just become slow or noisy.
It becomes entirely impractical.

## ICMP Oracle assisted traversal

The [ICMP Oracle](/ORACLE-NAT-HP.md) can help with a small modification.
Instead of using a known endpoint on the second peer device, an assumed port can be picked first.
That port needs to have a non-zero chance of being allocated by the second peer device.

The ICMP Oracle can then reveal the external translation endpoint on the first peer device.
Once this endpoint is known, it can be kept alive by sporadic transmissions from the first peer device.
That means the second peer device now has time to work out its part of the solution.

By varying its local ports, the second peer can cause the second NAT device to allocate distinct external translation ports until one of them eventually matches the previously assumed one.
Once this happens, both translation mappings are in place and the direct connection can be made.

## Time and packet count

The presented method should be able to solve the first part of the problem in less than 30 seconds, with 256 outgoing transport-layer packets and fewer than 2048 ICMP probe packets.

Then comes the slow and boring part of the problem.
Its requirements vary greatly depending on the number of possible ports that can be allocated by the second NAT device.
In the most difficult cases, this may mean up to 63k[^2] available ports.
At a modest rate of 100 packets per second, finding a matching port on the second NAT device would take almost 11 minutes.
On the other hand, a NAT device that allocates randomly from a pool of 64 ports would take around a second.

## Peer-isolated variant

There is also a variant where compatible mappings are created on the involved NAT devices, but no connection-setup packet travels all the way to the opposite NAT device.
Only traffic from the ICMP Oracle is needed to prepare the translation mappings.

To achieve this, the initial translation-mapping-creating packets from the first peer are sent with a limited TTL value.
In the second stage, the ICMP Oracle sends a slow stream of ICMP probes[^3] towards the second NAT device, while the second peer creates mappings with limited TTL as well.
Once a compatible mapping is created, the ICMP probes from the ICMP Oracle start reaching the second peer.
That is the signal that the translation mapping is ready to use.
